====== math.SphericalBesselJ ======

###
//math.SphericalBesselJ(l, x)// returns the spherical Bessel function of the first kind $j_l(x)$,
$$
j_l(x) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}} \, J_{l+1/2}(x),
$$
with $J$ the Bessel function. The first two are $j_0(x) = \sin(x)/x$ and $j_1(x) = \sin(x)/x^2 - \cos(x)/x$. The spherical Bessel functions appear, for example, in the expansion of a plane wave in spherical harmonics, $e^{i \mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{r}} = 4\pi \sum_{lm} i^l j_l(kr) Y_{lm}^*(\hat{k}) Y_{lm}(\hat{r})$, and in the integrals of [[documentation:language_reference:functions:getmultipolebesselintegral|]].
###

===== Input =====

  * //l// : Integer, the order, $l \ge 0$.
  * //x// : Real, the argument; for $x < 0$, $j_l(-x) = (-1)^l j_l(x)$.
  * Options : (optional) A table of options. Possible options are:
      * //"Method"// : String, //"Recurrence"// (default) or //"Series"//. //"Recurrence"// is accurate to about $10^{-13}$ (relative), also for large $l$. //"Series"// is the method of earlier versions of Quanty, two series expansions, one for small and one for large $x$. For large $l$ it is inaccurate in between, for $x$ near $l/2$. <color #ed1c24>New in 0.9.1</color>

===== Output =====

  * Real, the value $j_l(x)$.

===== Example =====

###
$j_0$ and $j_1$ compared with their closed forms. At $x = 0.001$ the closed form of $j_1$ loses digits, as two almost equal terms are subtracted; the exact value is $j_1(0.001) = 3.33333300000001 \cdot 10^{-4}$. At $x = 0$, $j_0 = 1$ and $j_l = 0$ for $l > 0$.
###

==== Input ====
<code Quanty Example.Quanty>
-- j0 and j1 against their closed forms
print("   x        j0(x)              sin(x)/x           j1(x)              sin(x)/x^2-cos(x)/x")
for _, x in ipairs({0.001, 0.5, 2, 10, 100}) do
  local j0 = math.SphericalBesselJ(0, x)
  local j1 = math.SphericalBesselJ(1, x)
  print(string.format("%7.3f  %18.15f %18.15f %18.15f %18.15f", x, j0, math.sin(x)/x, j1, math.sin(x)/x^2 - math.cos(x)/x))
end
print(math.SphericalBesselJ(0, 0), math.SphericalBesselJ(3, 0))
</code>

###
The two methods for larger $l$, compared with values of $j_l(x)$ to 15 digits. For $l = 5$ they agree; for $l = 20$ and $l = 30$ near $x = l/2$ the series loses accuracy.
###

==== Input ====
<code Quanty Example.Quanty>
-- the two methods for large l, against values of j_l(x) to 15 digits
for _, r in ipairs({{5, 2, 2.63516977024412e-3}, {20, 11.88, 4.33642532610588e-5}, {30, 17, 4.24200329973575e-7}}) do
  local l, x, exact = r[1], r[2], r[3]
  local rec = math.SphericalBesselJ(l, x)
  local ser = math.SphericalBesselJ(l, x, {{"Method", "Series"}})
  print(string.format("j_%d(%g) = %.14e   Recurrence %.14e   Series %.14e", l, x, exact, rec, ser))
end
</code>
