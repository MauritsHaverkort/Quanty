====== XCFunctional ======
;;#
<color #ed1c24>New in 0.9.1</color>
;;#

###
//e, dedn, dedsigma = XCFunctional(method, n, sigma)// evaluates an exchange-correlation functional of density functional theory at one point: the exchange-correlation energy per volume $e(n, \sigma)$ of a spin-unpolarized electron density $n$ with $\sigma = |\nabla n|^2$, and its two derivatives $\partial e/\partial n$ and $\partial e/\partial \sigma$. The exchange-correlation energy of a density $n(\mathbf{r})$ is
$$
E_{xc} = \int e\big(n(\mathbf{r}), \sigma(\mathbf{r})\big) \, \mathrm{d}^3 r.
$$
These are the functionals of the density functional methods of [[documentation:language_reference:functions:calculateatomicbasisfunctions|]] (option //"Method"//).
###

###
For the local density approximations (LDA) $e$ depends on $n$ only and $\partial e/\partial n$ is the exchange-correlation potential $V_{xc}$. For the gradient corrected functionals (PBE) the potential is
$$
V_{xc}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\partial e}{\partial n} - 2 \nabla \cdot \left( \frac{\partial e}{\partial \sigma} \nabla n \right).
$$
All quantities are in Hartree atomic units.
###

===== Input =====

  * //method// : String, the functional (upper or lower case):
      * //"LDA"// : Slater exchange and the correlation of Perdew and Wang (1992).
      * //"LDA-VWN"// : Slater exchange and the correlation of Vosko, Wilk and Nusair (VWN5). This is the LDA of the NIST atomic reference data.
      * //"RLDA-MV"// : as //"LDA-VWN"//, with the relativistic correction of MacDonald and Vosko to the exchange. This is the RLDA of the NIST atomic reference data. It uses the speed of light //PhysicalConstants.cAU.value//, see [[documentation:language_reference:constants:physical_constants|]].
      * //"PBE"// : the gradient corrected functional of Perdew, Burke and Ernzerhof.
      * //"PBE-VWN"// : PBE with the VWN5 correlation in place of the Perdew-Wang correlation under the gradient correction.
  * //n// : Real, the electron density in electrons per bohr$^3$.
  * //sigma// : (optional) Real, $\sigma = |\nabla n|^2$ in bohr$^{-8}$. Default value is 0. Only //"PBE"// and //"PBE-VWN"// use it.

===== Output =====

  * //e// : Real, the exchange-correlation energy per volume in Hartree per bohr$^3$. //e/n// is the energy per electron.
  * //dedn// : Real, $\partial e/\partial n$ in Hartree. For the LDAs this is the exchange-correlation potential.
  * //dedsigma// : Real, $\partial e/\partial \sigma$ in Hartree bohr$^5$. It is 0 for the LDAs.

###
For $n \le 0$ all three are 0.
###

===== Example =====

###
The uniform electron gas in LDA. Its density is given by the Wigner-Seitz radius $r_s$, $n = 3/(4\pi r_s^3)$. The exchange energy per electron is known in closed form, $\epsilon_x = -\frac{3}{4\pi} \left(\frac{9\pi}{4}\right)^{1/3} \frac{1}{r_s} = -0.458165/r_s$ Hartree, and is equal to the exchange part of //e/n//; the rest is the correlation energy per electron $\epsilon_c$. The Perdew-Wang and the VWN correlation are two fits to the same quantum Monte Carlo energies of Ceperley and Alder and agree to better than $10^{-3}$ Hartree.
###

==== Input ====
<code Quanty Example.Quanty>
-- the exchange-correlation energy of the uniform electron gas in LDA
-- rs is the Wigner-Seitz radius in bohr, n = 3/(4 pi rs^3) the density in bohr^-3
print("  rs      n         eps_xc     eps_x      eps_c     eps_c(VWN)   v_xc")
for _, rs in ipairs({0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10}) do
  local n = 3/(4*math.pi*rs^3)
  local e, dedn = XCFunctional("LDA", n)
  local eVWN = XCFunctional("LDA-VWN", n)
  local epsx = -3/(4*math.pi)*(9*math.pi/4)^(1/3)/rs   -- Slater exchange per electron
  print(string.format("%5.1f  %9.6f  %9.6f  %9.6f  %9.6f  %9.6f  %9.6f", rs, n, e/n, epsx, e/n - epsx, eVWN/n - epsx, dedn))
end
</code>

###
All functionals without a density gradient. With $\sigma = 0$ //"PBE"// is equal to //"LDA"// and //"PBE-VWN"// to //"LDA-VWN"//. The relativistic correction of //"RLDA-MV"// depends on $k_F/c$, with the Fermi wave vector $k_F = (3\pi^2 n)^{1/3}$. It is small at ordinary densities. At the very high densities near the nucleus of the heaviest atoms it reduces the exchange energy to a few percent of its nonrelativistic value, and the potential becomes positive.
###

==== Input ====
<code Quanty Example.Quanty>
-- all functionals without a gradient (sigma = 0), at a low and at a very high density
print(string.format("c = %.6f, the speed of light used by RLDA-MV", PhysicalConstants.cAU.value))
for _, n in ipairs({1, 1e6}) do
  local kF = (3*math.pi^2*n)^(1/3)
  print(string.format("n = %g bohr^-3, kF/c = %.4f", n, kF/PhysicalConstants.cAU.value))
  for _, method in ipairs({"LDA", "LDA-VWN", "RLDA-MV", "PBE", "PBE-VWN"}) do
    local e, dedn = XCFunctional(method, n)
    print(string.format("  %-8s e/n = %11.6f   de/dn = %11.6f", method, e/n, dedn))
  end
end
</code>

###
PBE with a density gradient, given by the reduced gradient $s = |\nabla n|/(2 k_F n)$, so $\sigma = (2 k_F n s)^2$. The gradient lowers the energy below that of the LDA. The derivative //dedsigma// is compared with a finite difference. At $\sigma = 0$ it is 0: in PBE the gradient corrections of exchange and correlation cancel for a slowly varying density.
###

==== Input ====
<code Quanty Example.Quanty>
-- PBE at n = 0.1 bohr^-3 for a few reduced gradients s = |grad n|/(2 kF n)
n = 0.1
kF = (3*math.pi^2*n)^(1/3)
eLDA = XCFunctional("LDA", n)
e, dedn, dedsigma = XCFunctional("PBE", n, 0)
print(string.format("sigma = 0: e/e_LDA = %.5f, de/dn = %.6f, de/dsigma = %.6f", e/eLDA, dedn, dedsigma))
print("  s     sigma     e/e_LDA     de/dn     de/dsigma  finite difference")
for _, s in ipairs({0.5, 1, 2, 5}) do
  local sigma = (2*kF*n*s)^2
  local e, dedn, dedsigma = XCFunctional("PBE", n, sigma)
  local h = 1e-4*sigma
  local fd = (XCFunctional("PBE", n, sigma + h) - XCFunctional("PBE", n, sigma - h))/(2*h)
  print(string.format("%4.1f  %8.5f  %9.5f  %10.6f  %10.6f  %10.6f", s, sigma, e/eLDA, dedn, dedsigma, fd))
end
</code>
